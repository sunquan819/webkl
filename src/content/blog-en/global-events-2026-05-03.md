May 3, 2026 marked several significant global developments across geopolitics, business, and technology.

## Iran Peace Proposal

Iran submitted a 14-point response to the US proposal to end the ongoing conflict. This marks a potential diplomatic breakthrough after months of escalation.

The details remain confidential, but sources suggest Iran is seeking guarantees about future sanctions relief and regional security arrangements. Trump expressed cautious optimism but said the US is "not satisfied yet" with Iran's latest proposal.

The war has lasted over 60 days now, affecting global oil prices and shipping routes through the Strait of Hormuz. Any de-escalation would relieve pressure on energy markets.

## US Troop Withdrawal from Germany

Germany stated that US troop withdrawal is "foreseeable" as NATO seeks clarification on the timeline.

The US plans to withdraw 5,000 troops from Germany over 6-12 months. This follows Trump's frustration with European NATO members over Iran war contributions.

German officials have increased military spending significantly, making them less dependent on US forces. But the withdrawal still signals shifting US priorities toward the Middle East.

This affects European security architecture and US-German relations at a critical moment.

## Spirit Airlines Shutdown

Spirit Airlines announced it will cease operations after failing to secure a government rescue deal.

The low-cost carrier struggled with high oil prices and reduced travel demand. The Iran war pushed jet fuel costs to unprecedented levels, making budget airline operations unsustainable.

This leaves millions of travelers with fewer options and raises questions about airline industry consolidation. Competitors like Southwest and Frontier may absorb some routes, but pricing will likely increase.

## Berkshire Hathaway's Transition

Berkshire Hathaway's new CEO Greg Abel took the stage, making a case for the post-Buffett era.

At the annual shareholder meeting, Buffett's "digital avatar" appeared, using AI technology to interact with attendees. Abel warned about cybersecurity risks and emphasized Berkshire's diversified approach.

Abel confirmed Berkshire's $4.3 billion Alphabet investment, signaling a shift toward tech under his leadership. Buffett's retirement in less than two weeks marks the end of a 60-year legacy.

Shareholders seem cautiously optimistic about Abel's leadership.

## California Water Crisis

California, Arizona, and Nevada announced a new water-saving plan for the dwindling Colorado River.

The river supplies water to 40 million people across the Southwest. Years of drought and climate change have reduced flows dramatically. The new plan involves voluntary conservation measures and federal funding for water recycling.

Without intervention, Lake Mead and Lake Powell could reach critical levels, threatening hydroelectric power and water supply.

This represents regional cooperation on an existential issue.

## Taiwan President's Africa Visit

Taiwan's President William Lai landed in eSwatini during a trip that Beijing calls a political "stunt."

eSwatini is one of Taiwan's few remaining diplomatic allies. China views Taiwan's diplomatic activities as provocations, especially during sensitive periods.

The visit underscores Taiwan's efforts to maintain international presence despite Chinese pressure. Beijing continues to isolate Taiwan diplomatically, reducing its official allies to a handful.
